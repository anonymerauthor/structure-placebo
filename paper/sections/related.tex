\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We organise the literature around one question: when a population method
attributes its performance to a score, what evidence is offered, and what would
settle the matter?

\emph{Topology and weights as design choices.} That the interaction graph
shapes behaviour is well established. Kennedy and Mendes found particle swarm
performance to depend systematically on the communication
topology~\cite{kennedy2002population}; the fully informed
variant~\cite{mendes2004fully} and comprehensive learning~\cite{liang2006clpso}
pursue the same insight through the update rule, and decentralised differential
evolution through the population graph~\cite{dorronsoro2011cellular}. Cellular
evolutionary algorithms make the mechanism explicit: Alba and Dorronsoro
parameterise the neighbourhood--grid relationship by a ratio, show it governs
selection pressure, and schedule it during the
run~\cite{alba2005exploration}. This line establishes (P1) of
Section~\ref{sec:claim}---heterogeneity and connectivity matter---but makes no
claim of the form (P2): no node is asserted to be more informative by virtue of
its position.

A more recent family does make such a claim, weighting the update by a
structural statistic computed on the population graph. The graphs are typically
Watts--Strogatz small worlds~\cite{watts1998collective} and the statistic
typically betweenness, computable by Brandes'
algorithm~\cite{brandes2001faster}. Our primary case study places candidates on
such a graph and weights influence by betweenness, arguing that
high-betweenness agents bridge regions of the search
space~\cite{jalali2026social}. Our concern is not whether these methods perform
competitively but whether the statistic is the reason.

\emph{Topology as a measured object.} A parallel line observes rather than
designs. Oliveira, Bastos-Filho and Menezes reconstruct influence graphs from
the exchanges that occur during a run and characterise swarms through
network-science statistics, using them to diagnose
stagnation~\cite{oliveira2015network,oliveira2020uncovering}. Local optima
networks~\cite{ochoa2008lon} apply the same instinct to the landscape; as their
authors stress, the objective is characterisation, and the graph is built after
the fact. Such studies establish that graph statistics \emph{correlate} with
behaviour. They do not establish that feeding one back into the update helps,
which is a causal claim of a different order and the one we test.

\emph{Theory of consensus-type updates.} Consensus-based optimization provides
the most developed theory for updates of the form \eqref{eq:master}, with
global convergence established in the mean-field
limit~\cite{pinnau2017consensus,carrillo2018analytical} and for the
finite-particle scheme~\cite{fornasier2024cbo}. Polarized CBO localises the
consensus through a kernel~\cite{bungert2024polarized}; algebraically this is a
neighbourhood aggregate with a state-dependent weighting. This work
characterises what such operators \emph{do}. It takes the weighting as given
and does not ask whether one weighting is more informative than another with
the same distribution, which is what Proposition~\ref{prop:mean} addresses.

\emph{Adaptive components.} Adaptive operator selection formulates the choice
among components as a bandit problem, assigning credit from observed
improvement~\cite{fialho2010analyzing}. Outside metaheuristics, choosing a
graph to optimise a spectral property is routine~\cite{ghosh2006growing}, but
there the objective is stated and success is verifiable against it. Inside a
stochastic optimizer, the claim that selection is informed is seldom separated
from the effect of switching at all; our second control targets that gap.

\emph{Methodological critique.} S\"orensen's critique of metaphor-driven
design~\cite{sorensen2015metaheuristics} and subsequent programmatic
work~\cite{swan2022metaheuristics} have made component-level analysis common,
and protocols such as CEC2017~\cite{awad2016cec2017} have standardised outcome
measurement, alongside guidance on statistical
practice~\cite{derrac2011practical,garcia2009study}, sample
sizing~\cite{campelo2019sample} and benchmarking~\cite{bartz2020benchmarking}.

Ablation nonetheless answers an adjacent question. Deleting a component yields
a different algorithm, with a different operator spectrum and often a different
runtime, so the comparison confounds (P1) with (P2). Controls of the second
kind---mechanism intact, only the information it consumes destroyed---are long
established for testing whether an observed structure carries signal, from
degree-preserving rewiring in network biology~\cite{maslov2002specificity} to
null models in community ecology~\cite{gotelli2000null}, and comparable reality
checks have reshaped reported progress in neighbouring
fields~\cite{musgrave2020metric,lipton2019troubling}. We are not aware of their
systematic use to audit score-weighting claims in metaheuristics, and it is
this transfer, with the invariance properties that make it sound, that we
contribute.
