{Permutation Controls for Score-Weighted Metaheuristics}

\begin{abstract}
Many population-based optimizers weight interactions by a per-neighbour
score---a centrality on the population graph, the quality of a neighbour's
best---claiming the score identifies informative agents. The usual evidence is
an ablation, which cannot establish that claim: deleting the weighting changes
the mixing operator as well as the information it carries. We separate the two
by permuting the score across nodes, leaving the mechanism at full strength and
destroying only the correspondence between a node's position and its weight. We
prove this control equals uniform mixing in expectation while retaining the
original's weight heterogeneity pathwise, and bound when the comparison is
informative at all. On a published betweenness-weighted optimizer the mechanism
proves active yet uninformative: over $104$ function comparisons on two suites,
three dimensions and five budgets, permutation changes nothing that survives
correction. Unchanged, the instrument returns four further outcomes, among them
a quality weighting in the fully informed particle swarm whose per-function
effects reach $|\delta|=1$ in both directions and cancel in the mean. A null
from a test with that range is a property of the mechanism, not of low power.
\end{abstract}
